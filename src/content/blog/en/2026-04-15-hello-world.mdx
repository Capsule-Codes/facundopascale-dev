I've wanted to write for years. Not for ego, not for personal branding, not because "you need a blog." I want to write because there are things I learned the hard way over months that could save you weeks. And because I'm tired of seeing the same shallow answers recycled in every YouTube tutorial.

## What this is about

This blog will cover what matters to me as a software architect and indie builder:

- **Architecture** — Clean, Hexagonal, Screaming. Not the academic theory, but how it actually plays out when you have a deadline, a team of three, and a PM breathing down your neck.
- **Craft** — The craft of writing software. Design patterns, testing, refactoring, code review. The stuff that separates a dev who "makes it work" from one who builds systems that survive time.
- **Tooling** — Strict TypeScript, Tailwind, LazyVim, Zellij, pnpm. Not product reviews — analysis of why certain tools fundamentally change how you work.
- **The business** — Because code lives inside a business. Estimates, technical decisions with commercial impact, when to say "no" to a feature, and why the best code is the code that never gets written.

## The philosophy

If you read my [about page](/en/about), you already know: **concepts before code**. I'm not going to publish "how to install X in 5 minutes" tutorials. There are thousands of those and they're all the same.

What you will find here are decisions. Why I chose this architecture over that one. What went wrong. What I'd do differently. All backed by real code from real projects.

<Callout type="info" title="On AI">
  Yes, I use AI to work. Every day. But like Tony Stark uses Jarvis — I direct, the AI executes. If
  you don't know what to ask it, or worse, you can't tell when it's lying to you, you're not using
  AI. It's using you. In this blog, when I use AI, I'll say so. And I'll explain the reasoning
  behind every decision, which is the one thing AI can't give you.
</Callout>

## Who this is for

If you're just starting out and want a step-by-step tutorial, this isn't your place. If you already have experience and want to go deeper, question your decisions, and see how someone who's been in the trenches for years thinks — stick around. It's going to be good.
